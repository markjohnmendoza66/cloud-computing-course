## Reflection

The multi-cloud exploration gave me a clearer idea of how different cloud providers solve similar computing problems. Among AWS, Azure, and Google Cloud, Azure impressed me the most because of its connection to Microsoft technologies and its ability to support organizations that already use Windows-based systems. I also learned that cloud platforms are much more than online storage because they provide complete environments for developing, deploying, securing, and managing applications.

AWS, Azure, and Google Cloud share many fundamental features. All three offer virtual machines, storage services, databases, networking, identity management, security, monitoring, and scalable infrastructure. They also provide services that allow organizations to avoid purchasing physical servers for every new project. The main differences are found in their service portfolios, management tools, integrations, pricing approaches, and areas of specialization.

Business requirements strongly influence cloud platform selection. A company should consider its current infrastructure, budget, application type, performance requirements, security policies, location of users, and expected growth. For example, an organization that depends heavily on Microsoft technologies may benefit from Azure, while a company working heavily with AI and data may find Google Cloud more suitable. AWS can be useful when an organization wants access to a very broad selection of cloud services.

Acting as a Cloud Solutions Consultant taught me that choosing a provider requires careful analysis rather than simply selecting the most popular platform. The solution should match the client's actual goals and technical requirements.

This mission also improved my GitHub portfolio because I added structured research, comparison tables, business recommendations, screenshots, and a Linux investigation. It helped me practice organizing technical information in Markdown while connecting Linux server knowledge with cloud infrastructure.
